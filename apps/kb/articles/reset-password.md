## Symptoms

- You can't remember your password.
- Windows or Microsoft 365 says your password has **expired** or **must be changed**.
- You changed your password and something (phone email, Wi-Fi) stopped working.

## Reset it yourself (self-service)

1. Go to the sign-in page of your work account (for Microsoft 365: `https://portal.office.com`).
2. Enter your work email and click **Forgot my password** / **Can't access your account?**.
3. Complete the verification — usually a code sent to your phone or the Authenticator app.
4. Choose a new password (see the rules below) and confirm it.
5. Sign in again on your computer. If you are working remotely, **connect to the VPN first**, then lock (`Windows + L`) and unlock your computer with the new password so it syncs.

> **Tip:** If the self-service page says you are not registered, you haven't set up your recovery methods yet. Open a ticket and IT will reset it for you after confirming your identity.

## Choosing a good password

- At least **12 characters**. A short sentence works well, e.g. `Blue-kettle-on-Tuesday!`
- Don't reuse a password from any personal account.
- Don't add `1`, `2`, `3` to your old password — attackers try that first.
- Consider a password manager if your company provides one.

## After changing your password, update it everywhere

Old passwords saved on other devices will keep failing and can **lock your account**. Update the password on:

- Email on your phone and tablet (see "Set up work email on your phone").
- Office Wi-Fi on your phone, if it uses your work login.
- Any mapped drives or saved credentials on your laptop.

## Still can't get in?

Open a ticket (ask a colleague to open it for you if you can't sign in at all) and include:

- Your full name and username / email.
- The exact error message you see.
- Whether you are in the office or remote.

IT will **never** ask you to send your password by email or chat. If someone does, it's a scam — see "How to recognize and report phishing".
