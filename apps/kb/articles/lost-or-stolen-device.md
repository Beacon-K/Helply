## Report it immediately

Open a ticket straight away — category **Security**, **Impact: Medium, Urgency: High** — and call IT if you can. Don't wait until you're "sure" it's gone; IT can always cancel the alarm if you find it.

Tell IT:

- **Which device** (laptop asset tag if you know it, or phone model).
- **When and where** you last had it.
- Whether it was **locked** / powered off and whether it had a password or PIN.
- Whether it contained **confidential files** stored locally (not in OneDrive/SharePoint).

## What IT will do

- **Sign you out everywhere** and reset your password, so nobody can use saved sign-ins.
- **Remotely lock or wipe** the device, or just the work data in work apps on a personal phone.
- **Check the encryption status** — encrypted laptops (e.g. BitLocker / FileVault) keep your data safe even if the device is stolen.
- Update the **asset register** and arrange a replacement.

## You should also

1. **Change your work password** from another device if IT hasn't already done so.
2. For a stolen device, **report it to the police** and note the report number — insurers and IT will ask for it.
3. For a personal phone with work apps: use **Find My** (iPhone) or **Find My Device** (Android) to locate, lock or wipe it, and contact your mobile carrier.

## Prevent problems before they happen

- Always lock your screen when stepping away: `Windows + L` or `Ctrl + Cmd + Q` on Mac.
- Never leave a laptop visible in a car.
- Save work files to **OneDrive or SharePoint**, not only on the laptop — then a lost laptop doesn't mean lost work.
