## What is phishing?

Phishing is a fake message — email, text, Teams chat or phone call — designed to trick you into **clicking a link, opening an attachment, entering your password, or sending money**. It is the most common way small businesses get hacked.

## Warning signs

- **Urgency or pressure:** "Your account will be closed today", "Pay this overdue invoice within 1 hour".
- **Unexpected requests for money or data:** gift cards, changed bank details for a supplier, payroll changes, "can you do me a quick favour?"
- **Sender doesn't quite match:** display name says "Microsoft" or your CEO, but the address is `microsoft-support@outlook-secure.co` or a personal Gmail.
- **Links that go somewhere else:** hover over a link (don't click) — the real address appears at the bottom of the window. Does it match the company it claims to be from?
- **Unexpected attachments**, especially `.zip`, `.html`, `.htm`, `.exe`, or Office files asking you to **"Enable content"** / **"Enable macros"**.
- **Sign-in pages reached from an email.** If an email asks you to "verify your account", go to the website yourself instead of clicking.
- **External sender warnings:** Outlook may show a banner saying the message came from outside the organization. Treat those with extra care if they claim to be internal.

> **Golden rule:** If a message asks for **money, passwords, or bank detail changes**, verify it through a **different channel** — call the person on a number you already know, not one in the email.

## How to report it

1. **Don't click links, open attachments, or reply.**
2. In Outlook, use the **Report** button (**Report phishing** / **Report message**) on the toolbar or in the message's `...` menu. This sends it to IT's security team and removes it from your inbox.
3. No report button? **Forward it as an attachment** to IT or open a ticket (category: Security) and attach it.
4. If the message went to several colleagues, mention it — IT can remove it from everyone's mailbox.

## Already clicked or entered your password?

Don't panic, and don't hide it — speed matters much more than blame. Follow "I clicked a suspicious link or entered my password" **right now**.

## Texts, calls, and Teams messages

The same rules apply. IT will **never** ask for your password or MFA code, and won't ask you to install remote-control software unless you opened a ticket first.
